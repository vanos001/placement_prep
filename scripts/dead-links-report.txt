WHAT PASS 4 TRIED, AND WHAT IT PROVED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Automated resolution was pushed until it stopped producing trustworthy
  results. Each strategy was measured, not assumed:

  1. Structural URL variants (case, extension, trailing slash, separators,
     locale/version segments): 2,740 candidates over 483 links -> 7 fixes.
     A second round with host-alias rewrites (/users/X -> /~X, etc.):
     638 candidates -> 0 fixes.
  2. Crossref / arXiv metadata matching against the citation titles of 88
     dead PDFs. Only matches at >=0.80 title similarity were accepted, and
     two further matches were rejected on inspection: a journal article
     standing in for a PhD thesis, and a paper whose result exponent
     (0.091n) differed from the cited one (0.054n). Net: 5 fixes.
  3. Sitemap harvesting. Of the 468 links then outstanding, 440 sit on hosts
     that still resolve, so the pages moved rather than the sites dying.
     Each live host's own sitemap.xml / MkDocs search index was harvested:
     184 hosts, 102,590 URLs. Title/slug matching at a strict threshold
     produced 16 trustworthy replacements.

  Every candidate was rejected unless it passed a same-document test: no
  ancestor/index pages, no locale swaps, no version downgrades, and the
  leading distinctive word of the dead URL had to survive. That bar is why
  the yields are small - at a looser threshold the matcher proposed a
  Japanese-locale page for an English one, mapped 'sed one-liners' to
  'awk one-liners', and offered an older release in place of a newer one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT PASS 6 TRIED, AND WHAT IT PROVED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Crossref-style metadata resolution moved to OpenAlex (Semantic Scholar
     returns HTTP 429 to scripted clients). 81 unresolved dead PDFs were
     queried by citation title; 5 resolved to a live copy of the same work
     and were applied after reading the citation context for each.
  2. GitHub: 11 dead repo/file URLs checked through the API. Renames and
     moved files resolved 3 (ongardie/dissertation, keigoi/ocaml-mpst,
     WASI specifications/wasi-0.2.0); archived or deleted repos did not.
  3. Wayback CDX replaced the availability API as the archive channel. It
     answers reliably one query at a time but rate-limits bulk sweeps, so
     it was used only for URLs that no live copy could replace (2 applied:
     libev, Padovani research page).
  4. Live-document moves: several hosts restructured without redirects
     (web.dev articles -> developer.chrome.com, Anthos -> GKE, kubectl.docs
     pages/ -> references/, kernelnewbies CamelCase -> /Documentation/*).
     Each successor was fetched and title-checked against the citation.
  5. Pruned comparison posts (AWS blog, Confluent, CNCF) have no successor.
     Where the underlying topic is still documented by the same vendor, the
     link now points at that vendor documentation and the label says so;
     nothing was pointed at a different document under the old label.
